It looks like you’re asking for **how to structure problem–solution SEO articles (1,000+ words) with FAQs at the end**. This is a very strong SEO format because it matches **how people actually search**: they have a problem, not a topic. Here’s a complete framework you can reuse for any niche. --- ## 🔷 What is a Problem–Solution SEO Article? This is an article built around: > **A specific user problem → clear explanation → step-by-step solution → FAQs** Instead of writing: > “Guide to Email Marketing” You write: > “Why Your Email Open Rates Are Low (And How to Fix Them)” That matches real search intent. --- ## 🔷 Why This Format Ranks Well Google prefers content that: * Matches **search intent** * Answers the **exact problem** * Keeps users on the page (high dwell time) * Covers related questions (FAQs → People Also Ask) * Is actionable, not theoretical This format hits all of those. --- ## 🔷 Ideal Structure (1,000–1,500 words) ### 1. Title (Problem-focused) Use this formula: > Why / How / What / Fix / Solve + Problem + Specific Context Examples: * Why Your Website Isn’t Ranking on Google (And How to Fix It) * How to Fix Slow Website Speed That’s Killing Your SEO * Why Your Blog Gets Traffic but No Conversions --- ### 2. Introduction (150–200 words) * Describe the problem * Show you understand the reader’s frustration * Promise a solution Example angle: > “If you’ve been publishing blog posts but still not ranking on Google, you’re not alone…” --- ### 3. Explain the Problem (200–300 words) * What is happening * Why it happens * Who it affects * Signs/symptoms Use subheadings like: * Common signs of this problem * Why most people get this wrong --- ### 4. The Root Causes (300–400 words) This is where SEO strength comes in. Break into causes: ``` H2: Cause #1 – … H2: Cause #2 – … H2: Cause #3 – … ``` Each cause should target related keywords. --- ### 5. Step-by-Step Solution (400–500 words) This is the core. ``` H2: Step 1 – … H2: Step 2 – … H2: Step 3 – … ``` Make it practical and actionable. --- ### 6. Common Mistakes to Avoid (150–200 words) This improves dwell time and adds semantic keywords. --- ### 7. Conclusion (100 words) Reinforce the solution and encourage action. --- ### 8. FAQs Section (Very important for SEO) Add 5–8 FAQs based on: * Google “People Also Ask” * Related searches * Real beginner questions Format: ``` H2: Frequently Asked Questions H3: Question? Answer (3–5 lines) ``` These often get featured snippets. --- ## 🔷 Example Outline **Topic:** Why Your Website Isn’t Ranking on Google 1. Intro – You’re publishing but not ranking 2. What ranking actually requires 3. Cause #1: No search intent match 4. Cause #2: Weak on-page SEO 5. Cause #3: No authority/backlinks 6. Step-by-step fix 7. Mistakes 8. FAQs --- ## 🔷 Keyword Strategy Inside the Article Instead of stuffing keywords, place them naturally in: * H2 headings * First 100 words * FAQs * Causes and steps Google reads structure, not density. --- ## 🔷 How FAQs Help SEO FAQs target: * Voice search * Featured snippets * Long-tail keywords * “People also ask” boxes They can bring 30–40% extra traffic alone. --- ## 🔷 Best Length * Minimum: **1,000 words** * Ideal: **1,200–1,500 words** Long enough to be authoritative, short enough to stay readable. --- ## 🔷 Template You Can Reuse ``` Title: [Problem] Intro – describe the pain H2: What is this problem? H2: Why this happens? H2: Cause #1 H2: Cause #2 H2: Cause #3 H2: Step-by-step solution H2: Mistakes to avoid H2: Frequently Asked Questions ``` --- ## 🔷 Examples of Problem–Solution Titles That Rank * Why Your Instagram Reels Get No Views * Why Your Shopify Store Has Traffic but No Sales * Why Your Emails Go to Spam * Why Your Website Loads Slowly * Why Your Facebook Ads Are Not Converting --- ## 🔷 Final Tip Always ask: > “What problem is the user trying to solve when they type this into Google?” That’s the article. --- If you want, tell me **your niche**, and I’ll create a **ready-to-publish 1,000+ word problem–solution article with FAQs** tailored to it.
